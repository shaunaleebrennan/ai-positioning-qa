# Worked example: SignalsDesk

SignalsDesk is fictional. The copy, company, product, and evaluation below exist only to demonstrate the framework.

## Context

- **Product:** An AI-assisted research repository for B2B product teams
- **Intended audience:** Product managers at growing B2B software companies
- **Buying trigger:** Customer evidence is scattered before a roadmap or positioning decision
- **Alternatives:** Spreadsheets, general-purpose note tools, manual synthesis, research repositories
- **Asset:** Homepage hero and supporting paragraph

## Assumptions and missing context

- No customer research, product documentation, proof points, or competitive evidence were supplied.
- The intended audience and buying trigger come from the fictional review brief, not from the source messaging itself.
- Product behaviour cannot be verified from the copy. Any proposed mechanism must remain a hypothesis until confirmed.

## Original messaging

> The future of customer intelligence is here.
>
> SignalsDesk is an innovative AI-powered platform that transforms your customer data into actionable insights, helping modern teams move faster and make smarter decisions.

## Summary assessment

The copy is readable but highly interchangeable. It does not identify a specific workflow, meaningful alternative, or credible reason to believe. The central opportunity is to replace category-level claims with a defined audience, buying situation, and product mechanism.

## Evaluation

| Dimension | Score | Evidence and rationale | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| ICP specificity | 1/5 | “Modern teams” does not identify the intended product-management audience or buying trigger. | High |
| Problem relevance | 2/5 | Scattered customer evidence is implied by “customer data,” but the workflow problem and consequence are absent. | Medium |
| Differentiation | 1/5 | “AI-powered,” “actionable insights,” and “move faster” could describe many products. | High |
| Value articulation | 2/5 | Faster, smarter decisions are outcomes, but no mechanism connects the product to them. | High |
| Clarity | 3/5 | The general category is understandable, but the actual input, output, and workflow remain unclear. | High |
| Evidence and credibility | 2/5 | “Transforms” and “smarter decisions” are unsupported and unbounded. | Medium |
| Narrative consistency | 4/5 | The short asset consistently discusses customer intelligence and decisions, despite being generic. | Medium |
| Responsible AI claims | 2/5 | AI is presented as a capability without explaining its role, oversight, or limitations. | High |

**Weighted score:** 39/100  
**Overall confidence:** High for copy quality; low for market relevance because no customer research was supplied.

## Highest-impact findings

1. Identify the product-management workflow and decision moment.
2. Explain what the product does with customer evidence instead of relying on “AI-powered.”
3. Replace the unsupported transformation promise with a specific, testable benefit.

## Illustrative revision

> Turn scattered customer conversations into evidence your product team can use.
>
> SignalsDesk brings interview notes, support themes, and sales feedback into one research trail. AI helps product managers group recurring needs and trace every finding back to its source before roadmap and positioning decisions.

This revision is illustrative, not validated positioning. It introduces three product hypotheses not established by the original brief: support for these specific inputs, AI grouping of recurring needs, and source-level traceability. Confirm those capabilities before using the copy. The revision should then be tested with intended users and compared with the alternatives they actually consider.

## Unsupported or risky claims

| Claim | Risk | Evidence or qualification needed |
|---|---|---|
| “The future of customer intelligence is here” | Unverifiable category superlative | Remove it or define the specific market change the product addresses |
| “Transforms your customer data” | Overstates an unspecified mechanism and result | Product behaviour, supported inputs, outputs, and limitations |
| “Actionable insights” | Generic and unsupported | A defined output, workflow, and example of the action it enables |
| “Move faster and make smarter decisions” | Unbounded performance claim | A specific decision, baseline, and credible customer or product evidence |

## Re-evaluation notes

The revision improves audience specificity, problem relevance, clarity, and responsible description of AI. It does not solve the lack of customer proof or establish that “one research trail” is meaningfully differentiated. Those require evidence, not more copy.

## Recommended next test

Show the original and revised message to five product managers who recently synthesized customer evidence. Ask each participant:

1. What do you think this product does?
2. Who is it for?
3. When would someone look for it?
4. What would you use instead?
5. Which claim would you need proven before considering it?

Code responses before deciding whether the revision is stronger.

## Human review notes

- **Accepted:** Narrow the audience and name the decision context.
- **Accepted with modification:** Explain a product mechanism only after Product confirms the supported inputs and traceability behaviour.
- **Rejected:** Treating the weighted score as evidence of message-market fit.
